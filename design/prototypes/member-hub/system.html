CNAP Member Hub · Design foundations, Round 4 draft for review

One design family, from the homepage to the audit log

These foundations are meant for every one of the Hub’s screens. The homepage uses them generously, while data tables and long reporting forms use the same tokens more densely.

Typography

One rule decides the family. Lora, the serif the Hub already uses, belongs to the Center’s science and people: story headlines, talk titles, and achievements. Inter belongs to you and your tools: greetings, tasks, navigation, forms, and tables. Reading text is at least 14 pixels, and only type labels and tags use 13.

Spacing and grid

A 4-pixel base. Twelve columns with 32-pixel gutters, at most 1,360 pixels wide including 40-pixel margins. One column with 20-pixel margins on phones. Sections are 88 pixels apart on desktop and 56 on phones. Every section header uses the same three-part row: heading in columns 1 to 3, one sentence in columns 4 to 9, and its link in columns 10 to 12.

Buttons and links

One primary action per area. Every control is at least 44 pixels tall where it stands alone. Focus is a 2-pixel purple ring with space around it.

Three levels, chosen by importance, not by section. Primary, filled purple: the one required or time-critical action in an area, such as Continue reporting. Secondary, outlined: a useful action the member chooses, such as Review the match. Tertiary, an underlined link: going somewhere to read more, such as Read the request. Every standalone action is 44 pixels tall, so actions in a row share one baseline.

Breakpoints, motion, and the gallery

Four layouts, tested at 1440, 1280, 1024, 768, 390, 360, and 320 pixels. Nothing scrolls sideways at any of them.

Above 1,180

Full header with six destinations. “Your week” is four columns, with the intro beside the items.

981 to 1,180

Search becomes an icon. The “Your week” intro becomes a header row above three item columns, so no column is narrower than 280 pixels.

980 and below

The bottom tab bar replaces the header links, so there’s always primary navigation. The page reserves 64 pixels plus the device’s safe area under the content, and scrolling to a focused element leaves 84 pixels clear above the bar. “Your week” becomes a list: the most urgent item opens in full, and the rest show a marker and a title on one tap target with a chevron.

640 and below

One column with 20-pixel margins. Lower sections show their two most useful items, with a link to the rest.

Motion

On an aubergine ground, the greeting’s words come into focus one at a time and the pathway lines draw in once. Then a few signals travel slowly along the right half of the pathways, never behind text. A Pause motion button stops everything, and with reduced motion turned on, the greeting appears still and the signals never start. Hovers change color and outline only, except a 2 percent zoom on gallery photographs.

Greeting

The greeting scales with the screen, from 88 pixels on desktop to 42 on small phones. Under it, one line gives the week of the semester and the days until the next break, from the K-State academic calendar. “Your week” sits on white below it.

Gallery

One lead photograph across eight columns at 3 to 2, and two supporting photographs stacked in four columns. On phones, the lead runs edge to edge at 4 to 3 and the two supporting photographs sit side by side as squares. Nothing plays on its own, every photograph is a link with its caption as the name, and placeholders stay labeled until a permitted photograph replaces them.

Type floor

Reading text is at least 14 pixels. Tags, tab labels, initials, and counts use 13. Nothing is smaller.

Status and type markers

Every status pairs a color with a word, and type markers add an icon. Nothing depends on color alone.

Bands, lists, and cards

Lists with hairlines are the default. The warm band marks two places only: your own items at the top, and the photo reel. Cards are kept for objects people compare side by side. Images, bands, and panels share one 4-pixel corner. Numbers mark only real sequences, such as the six stages of a proposal.

Forms and tables

The same type and lines, set denser. Labels sit above fields, help text sits below the label, and errors say how to fix the problem. Field borders are darker than dividers so a field’s edge meets the 3 to 1 contrast rule.
